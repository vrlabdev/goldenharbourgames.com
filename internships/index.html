Game development internships

Remote, part-time internships on real games that are already in the App Store and on Google Play. Pick a game, make one part of it better, and get your name in its credits.

What you get

  • Work on a released game. What you make ships to real players, usually within weeks.
  • Your name in the credits of the game and on this site, and a reference from us you can show anyone.
  • Something finished for your portfolio: a level, a character, a soundtrack, a feature, a language.
  • The full source code and our tools, including the AI tools we build with, and one of us in a chat when you are stuck.
  • A share of what the game earns, agreed in writing before you start.

How it works

  1. Choose a game and the part you want to work on. The open projects are listed here; your own idea is welcome too.
  2. Email us. Say what you would like to do and show us anything you have made, finished or not. We answer within a few days.
  3. We agree the project in writing: what it is, what finished looks like, how long it should take and what you get.
  4. You work remotely, in your own hours. Most projects are a few weeks of part-time work.
  5. Your work ships in the game, with your name on it.

Who can apply

Students, graduates, career changers and self-taught beginners, anywhere in the world. Game designers, programmers (we use Godot and Unity), 2D and 3D artists, animators, composers and sound designers, writers, translators, voice actors and testers. You do not need a degree or a portfolio. You need to care about one of these games enough to make one part of it better.

Questions people ask

Is the internship paid?

There is no salary. You get credit, a reference and portfolio work, and a share of the game’s earnings that we agree in writing before you start. Our games earn little so far, and we will tell you the real numbers in the first email. You never pay us anything.

Is it remote?

Yes, fully. The studio is in Sydney, Australia, and we work with people in any time zone. Everything happens over email, chat and the shared project.

How many hours is it?

As many as you agreed to. Most interns give five to ten hours a week around study or a job. There are no fixed hours.

Do I need experience?

No. We care more about what you want to make than about what you have done. If you are new to the tools, we show you how we use them.

Which tools do you use?

Godot 4 and Unity for the games, Blender for 3D, GitHub for everything, and AI tools such as Claude for code and first drafts. You will learn how a small studio ships with them.

Can it count for my course?

It can. Tell us what your school needs, such as a supervisor, hours or a report, and we will work out with you whether we can provide it.

When can I start?

Any time. There are no intakes. Email us and we go from there.
