Where to find us, and where we gave up

Every account the studio runs, what we post on each, the real numbers, the one platform we left, and the places where a message actually gets an answer.

A studio of two cannot be everywhere, and for a month we tried anyway. This is the map as it stands on 16 November: where we post, how often, how it is going in plain numbers, and where to write if you want a reply.

YouTube: the main channel

@goldenharbourgames. A hundred and thirty-one videos, a little over thirty-eight thousand views, twenty-three subscribers. Almost all of it is Shorts, and almost all of the views came from the Shorts feed, not from people who know us.

We post in two lanes. What changed: one problem in one game, the build before and the build after, forty to sixty seconds. And how it plays: one mechanic, shown in real play with the interface on. The trailers are there too.

What we learned: a short film about a real thing travels and a short film about our game does not. "Why is fire retardant pink?" got nearly two thousand views. A three-card knockout in Punch League got a hundred and eighteen. So the firefighting films explain firefighting, and the game is just where it is shown.

Instagram and Facebook: new this week

Instagram and the Facebook page opened on 13 November. Two Reels a day, morning and evening Sydney time, plus the trailers and the odd set of stills. Instagram has twenty-seven followers and Facebook has two, which is what three days looks like.

It has already done one thing YouTube had not: somebody asked a real question. Under the Russian trailer for Souls & Estates a player wanted to know whether towns grow by themselves and whether there is a tech tree. (Villages grow by themselves; you or your steward place the buildings; no tech tree.) One real question is worth more to us than a thousand views from a feed.

X

@GoldenHrbrGames. The same short films, and replies in the weekly threads where developers show each other what they are making. It is small, and it is mostly other developers. That suits us: they have the same problems we do.

Reddit

We are there as the studio, mostly in comments: answering questions about Godot exports, store review and small-studio problems where we have something real to say. Many game communities do not accept games made with AI tools. Ours are, and we say so, so we post our games only where the rules allow it and stay out of the rest.

itch.io

Every game has a page with open comments, and the bigger changes get a devlog there as well as here. For the browser builds it is also the fastest place to tell us something broke.

TikTok: we left

We posted there for three weeks. Some films reached a few hundred people, then every new one reached nobody, whatever we changed. One follower. We stopped on 9 November and deleted the queue. If you came here looking for our TikTok, that is why it is quiet.

Where a message gets an answer

  • Email: contact@goldenharbourgames.com. A person reads it.
  • The comments on a game’s itch.io page. Best for "this broke" and "this felt wrong".
  • The forum on this site, for longer talk about the games and the experiments.
  • Comments on Instagram and YouTube. We read them all. There are not so many yet that it is hard.

What we will try next

More of what gets a question, less of what gets a view. Short films about real things first. Devlogs like this one for people who care how games get made. Letters to people who make videos about the kinds of games we make, which has had more replies than we expected. And fewer platforms, done properly, over more platforms done by a queue.

If there is somewhere you would rather hear from us, say so. That is a real question, and we will move.

In active development

Every game here is still being built. We try things, keep what works and change them step by step. Tell us what you think: we read everything and act on it.
